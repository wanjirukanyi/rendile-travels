Nyungwe Forest: A Woman's Guide

A canopy walkway high above one of Africa's oldest rainforests, alive with chimps.

Home / Rwanda / Nyungwe Forest

In Rwanda's south-west lies Nyungwe, one of the oldest and largest mountain rainforests in Africa — a green ocean of ancient trees, waterfalls and orchids. Walk its famous canopy bridge, track chimpanzees through the mist, and watch great troops of colobus monkeys flow through the treetops.

Why visit Nyungwe?

Nyungwe National Park protects a remarkably intact slice of pre-Ice-Age rainforest, with thirteen primate species, hundreds of bird species and some 1,000 plant species. It's a complete change of mood from the volcanoes — cool, lush and primeval — and pairs beautifully with a gorilla trek and a stay on Lake Kivu.

The canopy walk

East Africa's only canopy walkway hangs sixty metres above the forest floor, a 200-metre suspension bridge with the treetops below and the hills of Burundi in the distance. It's an exhilarating, surprisingly serene way to experience the rainforest from the birds' point of view — and a highlight of any visit.

Primates & wildlife

  • Chimpanzees: an early-morning trek to track a habituated community through the forest.
  • Colobus monkeys: huge, spectacular super-troops of Angolan colobus — sometimes hundreds strong.
  • Birds & orchids: a top-tier birding site with Albertine Rift endemics, plus waterfalls and wild orchids.

Best time to visit

Nyungwe is rewarding year-round, but the drier months — June–September and December–February — make the forest trails firmest for chimp tracking. Being a rainforest, a shower is always possible, so pack a light waterproof. See our best time to visit Rwanda guide for more.

Where to stay & how to get there

We favour lodges set amid the tea estates on the forest edge, several with sweeping views over the canopy. Nyungwe is reached on the gorgeous Congo-Nile drive along Lake Kivu, or by a quick scheduled flight to the nearby airstrip. We arrange every leg and the permits.
