Serengeti Safari: A Woman's Guide

Endless plains, big cats and the home of the Great Migration.

Home / Tanzania / Serengeti

The Serengeti is safari at its most cinematic — grasslands stretching past the curve of the earth, lion prides on the kopjes, and over a million wildebeest moving with the rains. For women travelling through East Africa it is a bucket-list landscape made wonderfully easy by superb camps and warm Tanzanian hospitality.

Why visit the Serengeti?

Tanzania's oldest and most famous national park protects nearly 15,000 square kilometres of plains, woodland and river country — and one of the highest concentrations of large predators in Africa. It is the southern, larger half of the same ecosystem as Kenya's Maasai Mara, which means superb year-round game viewing and the stage for the greatest wildlife show on earth.

The Great Migration

The migration is a year-round cycle, not a single event. From roughly December to March the herds gather on the short-grass plains of the southern Serengeti to calve — hundreds of thousands of wildebeest born within weeks. As the plains dry they move north and west, reaching the Mara River around July to October for the dramatic crossings. We position you in the right region for the season, in camps that move with the herds.

Wildlife you'll see

  • Big cats: the Serengeti is lion country, with cheetah on the open plains and leopard draped over the kopjes and riverine trees.
  • The Big Five: lion, leopard, elephant and buffalo are regular; rhino are present but rare.
  • Plains game: wildebeest, zebra, giraffe, topi and gazelle in great numbers.
  • Birdlife: over 500 species, from ostrich and secretary birds to lilac-breasted rollers.

Best time to visit the Serengeti

June–October is the dry season — easy game viewing and the northern river crossings. January–March brings the calving on the southern plains, with predators in close attendance. The green months (April–May, November) are lush, quieter and excellent value. For a full month-by-month breakdown, see our best time to visit Tanzania guide.

Where to stay: women-friendly camps

We hand-pick intimate tented camps known for genuine warmth, safety and service — including seasonal mobile camps that follow the migration and permanent lodges in the central Seronera. For solo travellers we favour camps with a sociable communal table; for couples and privacy, smaller camps with fewer tents and unhurried sightings.

How to get there

Most guests fly by light aircraft from Arusha or Kilimanjaro to one of the Serengeti's airstrips — about one to two hours, with the plains unrolling below. The park also links overland to the Ngorongoro Crater and Tarangire on the classic northern circuit. We arrange every transfer, door to door.
