Why Uganda

The pearl of Africa.

Winston Churchill called Uganda "the pearl of Africa" — and a century on, it still dazzles. Almost half the world's mountain gorillas live in the misty Bwindi forest, chimpanzees call through Kibale's canopy, and tree-climbing lions doze in the fig trees of Queen Elizabeth. The Nile begins its long journey north at Jinja, and the south-west folds into emerald terraced hills.

For women travelling with us, Uganda is intimate and deeply rewarding: small lodges, warm hospitality, expert trackers, female guides where possible, and a Rendile team alongside you every step of the way.

Where to go

Uganda's highlights

Bwindi Impenetrable Forest

Almost half the world's mountain gorillas, tracked on foot through ancient rainforest.

Queen Elizabeth

Tree-climbing lions, elephant herds and the wildlife-lined Kazinga Channel.

Kibale Forest

The chimpanzee capital of East Africa, alive with thirteen primate species.

Murchison Falls

The Nile forced through a seven-metre gorge, then a wildlife-rich delta.

Lake Bunyonyi

Terraced hills around the "place of little birds" — among Africa's deepest lakes.

Jinja & the Nile

The source of the world's longest river, and East Africa's adventure capital.

When to go

Best time to travel

Jun – Sep · Dec – Feb

Dry-season trekking

Bwindi and Kibale's forest trails are firmest and gorilla tracking is at its easiest.

Jun – Aug

Peak game viewing

Thinner bush and water-drawn wildlife in Queen Elizabeth and Murchison Falls.

Year-round

Gorillas await

Permits run all year; the greener wet months are quieter, with fewer trekkers on the trail.
