Best Time to Visit Uganda

A season-by-season guide to trekking, game viewing and the green months.

Home / Uganda / Best time to visit

Uganda sits on the equator, so it's a year-round destination — gorilla permits are issued in every month. What shifts with the seasons is how easy the forest trails are, where the game gathers, and how green and quiet the country feels. Here's how to choose.

Uganda's seasons at a glance

There are two drier spells — June–September and December–February — and two wetter ones, the long rains (March–May) and the short rains (October–November). Being equatorial, Uganda is warm and green all year; even in the dry months a forest can deliver a sudden shower, so a light rain jacket is always worth packing.

Best for gorilla & chimp trekking

The drier months — June–September and December–February — are ideal for trekking in Bwindi and Kibale, when the steep forest trails are firmest and least muddy. Permits are limited and sell out months ahead in these peak windows, so we book early. Trekking has a minimum age of 15.

Best for game viewing

For the savanna parks — Queen Elizabeth and Murchison Falls — the dry months concentrate wildlife around the rivers and channels and keep the tracks easy. June to August in particular offers reliable big-game viewing alongside the boat safaris.

The green seasons

The wetter months (March–May and October–November) bring lush landscapes, dramatic skies, superb birding and noticeably fewer trekkers on the trail — and, often, lower lodge rates. Gorilla permits remain available, so flexible travellers who don't mind a muddier hike can have the forest almost to themselves.

When to think twice

The heart of the long rains (April–May) can make the forest trails genuinely demanding and some roads slow. For first-time visitors set on a comfortable trek we usually steer toward the dry windows — but with good boots and the right spirit, the green season can be wonderful and rewarding.
