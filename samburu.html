Samburu: Kenya's Wild North

Rare species and the proud Samburu people.

Home / Kenya / Samburu

North of the equator, where the savanna gives way to red earth and doum palms, Samburu feels wilder and more remote than Kenya's southern parks — home to species you'll see nowhere else, and to the Samburu, warm cousins of the Maasai.

Why visit Samburu?

Samburu National Reserve, on the banks of the Ewaso Ng'iro river in Kenya's arid north, offers dramatic scenery, excellent predator sightings and a real sense of frontier wilderness — with far fewer vehicles than the Mara.

The Samburu Special Five

Samburu is famous for five dry-country species found here and rarely elsewhere: the reticulated giraffe, Grevy's zebra, Beisa oryx, gerenuk (the "giraffe-necked" antelope) and Somali ostrich — alongside elephant, lion, leopard and cheetah.

The Samburu people

The Samburu are a semi-nomadic people known for their striking red dress and beadwork — the same beadwork that inspired our brand. We arrange respectful, women-led visits and meetings with Samburu women's cooperatives, so your visit gives back.

Best time to visit

The dry seasons (June–October, December–March) concentrate wildlife along the river and offer the best game viewing. See our best time to visit Kenya guide.

Where to stay

We favour intimate riverside camps and conservancy lodges with female guides, walking safaris and genuine community ties.

How to get there

A short charter flight from Nairobi, or a scenic drive past Mount Kenya. Samburu pairs wonderfully with the Mara and Laikipia for a wilder, off-the-beaten-track journey.
