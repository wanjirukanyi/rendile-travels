Bwindi Impenetrable Forest: A Woman's Guide

Almost half the world's mountain gorillas, tracked on foot through ancient rainforest.

Home / Uganda / Bwindi Impenetrable Forest

There is no wildlife encounter on earth quite like sitting a few metres from a wild mountain gorilla family — a silverback at rest, mothers nursing, youngsters tumbling through the undergrowth. Bwindi, a dense and ancient rainforest in south-west Uganda, is home to almost half the world's remaining mountain gorillas, and it is one of the most moving things a traveller can do.

Why visit Bwindi?

The Bwindi Impenetrable Forest is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the most biodiverse places in Africa — over 25,000 years old, draped in vines and ferns, and alive with birds and primates. Its name is well earned, but the reward at the end of the trek is unforgettable: a regulated hour in the presence of a habituated gorilla family.

Gorilla trekking: what to expect

After an early briefing you set out with a ranger and trackers who radio the gorillas' location. The hike can take anywhere from one to six hours over steep, muddy, beautiful terrain — then, suddenly, you are with them. You spend exactly one hour at a respectful distance before leaving them in peace. It is humbling, emotional and over far too soon.

Permits & how hard it is

A Uganda gorilla permit is required and strictly limited — we book yours well in advance, as they sell out months ahead in peak season. Trekking suits reasonably fit travellers aged 15 and over; local porters are available (and warmly recommended) to carry your daypack and lend a hand on the slopes. Go at your own pace — there is no rush.

Beyond the gorillas

  • Other primates: habituated groups aside, Bwindi shelters chimpanzees, colobus and L'Hoest's monkeys.
  • Birding: a world-class site with around 350 species, including many Albertine Rift endemics.
  • Culture: Batwa cultural experiences share the heritage of the forest's first people.

Best time to visit

Gorilla permits are available year-round, but trekking is easiest in the drier months — June–September and December–February — when the forest trails are firmest. The greener wet seasons are lush and quieter, with fewer trekkers on the trail. See our best time to visit Uganda guide for more.

Where to stay & how to get there

We choose intimate forest-edge lodges close to whichever trailhead (Buhoma, Ruhija, Rushaga or Nkuringo) your permit is assigned. Most guests fly by light aircraft to a Bwindi airstrip; the forest can also be reached overland from Entebbe or, conveniently, from Kigali in neighbouring Rwanda. We arrange every leg, door to door.
