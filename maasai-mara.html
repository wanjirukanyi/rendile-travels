Maasai Mara Safari: A Woman's Guide

Kenya's greatest reserve — the Great Migration, big cats and golden plains.

Home / Kenya / Maasai Mara

The Maasai Mara is the safari most travellers picture when they close their eyes — endless honey-coloured plains, lions at dawn, and a sky that goes on forever. For women travelling through East Africa, it is also one of the most rewarding and reassuringly easy places to begin.

Why visit the Maasai Mara?

The Maasai Mara National Reserve, in south-western Kenya, is the northern tip of the same ecosystem as Tanzania's Serengeti. It packs an extraordinary density of wildlife into rolling grassland, which means more sightings, in better light, with less driving. Add warm Maasai hospitality and some of Africa's finest tented camps, and it's easy to see why the Mara is the heart of almost every Kenya itinerary.

The Great Migration

From roughly July to October, more than a million wildebeest and hundreds of thousands of zebra pour into the Mara from the Serengeti, braving crocodile-filled rivers in the famous Mara River crossings. It is widely called the greatest wildlife spectacle on earth — and watching it from a private vehicle, coffee in hand, is unforgettable. Because these months are popular, the best camps book six to twelve months ahead.

Wildlife you'll see

  • Big cats: the Mara is famous for lion prides, cheetah on the plains and elusive leopard.
  • The Big Five: lion, leopard, elephant and buffalo are regularly seen; rhino are present but rarer.
  • Plains game: giraffe, zebra, topi, gazelle and resident wildebeest year-round.
  • Birdlife: over 450 species, from lilac-breasted rollers to secretary birds.

Best time to visit the Maasai Mara

July–October is peak season for the Great Migration and river crossings. January–March is warm, dry and quieter, with superb big-cat sightings and newborn wildlife. The long rains (April–May) bring lush, green, low-season value but some camps close. Whenever you travel, mornings and late afternoons offer the best game viewing and the softest light.

Where to stay: women-friendly camps

We hand-pick small tented camps and lodges known for genuine warmth, safety and service — many with female guides and staff. For solo travellers we favour camps with a sociable communal table; for privacy, intimate conservancy camps just outside the main reserve offer night drives, walking safaris and far fewer vehicles at each sighting.

Is the Maasai Mara safe for women travellers?

Yes. On safari you are with a professional guide at all times, in vetted camps, with a Rendile concierge reachable around the clock. Thousands of women — solo, with friends, mothers and daughters — safari in the Mara every year. We simply remove the guesswork: trusted camps, private transfers, and itineraries paced to how you want to feel.

How to get there

Most guests fly from Nairobi's Wilson Airport to one of the Mara's airstrips in about 45 minutes — a beautiful, time-saving hop over the Rift Valley. Road transfers are possible (around 5–6 hours) and can be combined with a stop at the Great Rift Valley viewpoints. We arrange every leg, door to door.
