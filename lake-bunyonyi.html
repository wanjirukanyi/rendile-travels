Lake Bunyonyi: A Woman's Guide

Terraced hills around the "place of little birds" — among Africa's deepest lakes.

Home / Uganda / Lake Bunyonyi

After the effort and emotion of a gorilla trek, there is no lovelier place to land than Lake Bunyonyi. Cradled by steeply terraced hills and dotted with twenty-nine green islands, its still, bilharzia-free water is one of the most beautiful — and most peaceful — corners of Uganda.

Why visit Lake Bunyonyi?

"Bunyonyi" means "place of little birds", and the name suits this serene highland lake perfectly. Believed to be among the deepest in Africa, it has no hippos or crocodiles, so you can swim, paddle and simply slow down. It's the natural restful finale to a south-west Uganda safari, an easy add-on after Bwindi.

Things to do

  • Dugout canoes: paddle a traditional canoe between the islands, each with its own story.
  • Village & community walks: meet local farmers and Batwa communities on the terraced slopes.
  • Birding & swimming: herons, weavers and kingfishers, and a rare safe-to-swim African lake.

Best time to visit

Bunyonyi is lovely year-round, but it pairs most naturally with gorilla trekking in the drier months — June–September and December–February. The green seasons bring misty, romantic mornings over the water. See our best time to visit Uganda guide for more.

Where to stay & how to get there

We favour calm, characterful lodges set on the shore or perched on the hillsides with long views over the islands. Bunyonyi is a short, scenic drive from Bwindi, which makes it the perfect place to unwind for a day or two before flying home from the nearby Kihihi airstrip — or crossing to Kigali. We arrange every leg.
