Lamu & the Swahili Coast

Old towns, dhow sails and barefoot Indian-Ocean days.

Home / Kenya / Lamu & the Coast

Where the savanna ends, the Swahili coast begins — warm turquoise water, white sand, and the timeless island of Lamu, a place with no cars, only donkeys, dhows and centuries of culture. It's the perfect, soulful end to a Kenya safari.

Why visit Lamu & the coast?

Kenya's Indian-Ocean coast offers some of Africa's loveliest beaches and richest culture. Lamu, the jewel, blends Swahili, Arab and Indian heritage into something utterly its own — slow, gracious and deeply photogenic.

Lamu Old Town (UNESCO)

Lamu Old Town is a UNESCO World Heritage Site — the oldest, best-preserved Swahili settlement in East Africa. Wander coral-stone alleys, carved doorways and bustling markets, with no traffic to break the spell.

Dhows & the ocean

Sailing a traditional wooden dhow at sunset is the quintessential Lamu experience. Add snorkelling, mangrove channels and fresh seafood feasts on the sand.

Barefoot beaches

Shela's long, empty beach is the coast's most famous, but we can also set you on Diani's powder sand further south, or whisk you to a private island for total seclusion.

The Lamu Yoga Festival

Each year Lamu hosts the joyful Lamu Yoga & Wellbeing Festival — five days of beachfront yoga, dhow cruises and Swahili feasts. It's one of our most-loved wellness trips.

Best time to visit

The coast shines December–March (hot, sunny, calm seas) and again July–October. The long rains (April–May) are quieter. See our best time to visit Kenya guide.

Where to stay & how to get there

We book beautiful Swahili houses and boutique hotels, all women-friendly. Lamu is a short flight from Nairobi or Mombasa, with a boat transfer to the islands — we handle every step.

End your safari by the sea

We'll pair the bush with barefoot coast days, designed around you.
