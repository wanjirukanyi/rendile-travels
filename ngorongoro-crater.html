Ngorongoro Crater: A Woman's Guide

A collapsed volcano sheltering the Big Five in one extraordinary bowl.

Home / Tanzania / Ngorongoro Crater

Drive over the rim at dawn and the whole of the Ngorongoro Crater opens beneath you — a green amphitheatre two thousand feet deep, dotted with elephant, lion and flamingo-pink lakes. It is one of the most concentrated wildlife spectacles on earth, and one of the easiest, most rewarding days on any Tanzania safari.

Why visit the Ngorongoro Crater?

The crater is the world's largest intact volcanic caldera — the collapsed cone of a once-vast mountain, now a self-contained sanctuary on the floor of the Ngorongoro Conservation Area, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Around 25,000 large animals live within its walls year-round, which means a near-guaranteed Big Five day in a single, breathtaking setting.

A day on the crater floor

You descend a steep track at sunrise into a mosaic of grassland, swamp, soda lake and acacia forest. Resident lion prides, huge-tusked elephant bulls, hippo pools and flamingo flocks are all within a compact area, so the driving is gentle and the sightings come thick and fast. By midday you climb back to the cool, forested rim for lunch with a view.

Wildlife you'll see

  • Black rhino: the crater is one of the best places in Tanzania to see this rare, critically endangered animal.
  • Lion & elephant: famous resident prides and some of Africa's most impressive old elephant bulls.
  • Flamingos & hippo: Lake Magadi turns pink with flamingos; hippo wallow in the central pools.
  • Plains game: wildebeest, zebra, buffalo, gazelle and the elegant golden jackal.

Best time to visit

Because the wildlife is resident, the crater is rewarding year-round. The dry season (June–October) gives clear skies and easy tracks; the green months are lush and quieter, and pair beautifully with the wildebeest calving on the nearby Ndutu plains (January–March). See our best time to visit Tanzania guide for the full picture.

Beyond the crater

The wider conservation area rewards a longer stay: Olduvai Gorge, the "cradle of humankind" where the Leakeys unearthed early human fossils; the calving plains of Ndutu; and the remote Empakaai Crater with its flamingo lake. Maasai still graze their cattle across these highlands, and a respectful village visit is easily arranged.

Where to stay & how to get there

We favour crater-rim lodges with sweeping caldera views and warm highland comfort, or characterful lodges in the coffee-green Karatu hills just below. Most guests arrive by road from Arusha (about three to four hours) or as part of the classic northern circuit linking Tarangire and the Serengeti. We arrange every transfer and the crater service fees.
