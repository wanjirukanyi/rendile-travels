Best Time to Visit Tanzania

A month-by-month guide to the migration, the seasons and the coast.

Home / Tanzania / Best time to visit

There's no single "best" month for Tanzania — it depends on what you most want to see. The dry season is easiest for game viewing, the green season is lush and quieter, and the Great Migration moves through the Serengeti all year. Here's how to choose, and how we time a journey around you.

Tanzania's seasons at a glance

Tanzania has two dry spells and two wet ones: the long dry season (June–October), the short rains (November), a warm dry window (January–February), and the long rains (April–May). The north (Serengeti, Ngorongoro) is a year-round safari region; the timing simply shifts where the wildlife — and the crowds — gather.

June–October: the dry season

The classic safari window. Vegetation thins, animals concentrate around water, skies are clear and the famous Mara River crossings unfold in the northern Serengeti (roughly July–October). It's peak season, so the best camps book six to twelve months ahead. This is also the prime window for climbing Kilimanjaro and for the Zanzibar beaches.

January–March: calving & green

A beautiful, warmer alternative. The short rains have passed, the south Serengeti plains are green, and from late January to March hundreds of thousands of wildebeest calve on the Ndutu plains — an extraordinary spectacle, with predators close behind. January–February is also a quieter window to climb Kilimanjaro and enjoy the coast.

The Great Migration, month by month

  • Dec–Mar: calving on the southern Serengeti / Ndutu plains.
  • Apr–May: the herds move north-west with the long rains (low season).
  • Jun–Jul: the columns push toward the western corridor and the Grumeti.
  • Jul–Oct: the dramatic Mara River crossings in the far north.

Because the herds are always somewhere, we simply place you in the right region for your dates — see the Serengeti guide for more.

Best time for the beach

Zanzibar and the Tanzanian coast are sunniest and driest from June–October and December–February. These windows pair perfectly with a northern-circuit safari for a savanna-to-sea journey. We avoid the long rains of April–May for beach time.

When to think twice

The long rains (April–May) bring lush scenery and the lowest prices, but also heavy downpours, some camp closures and tougher roads. For first-time visitors we usually steer toward the dry or calving seasons — though green-season value can be wonderful for flexible, return travellers.
