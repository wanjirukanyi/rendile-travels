Mount Kilimanjaro: A Woman's Guide

The snow-capped roof of Africa — trek it, or simply watch it glow at dawn.

Home / Tanzania / Mount Kilimanjaro

Rising alone from the plains to 5,895 metres, Kilimanjaro is the highest mountain in Africa and the tallest free-standing peak on earth — snow on the equator, and a summit thousands of ordinary, determined people reach every year. Whether you climb it or admire it from the savanna, it is unforgettable.

Can I climb Kilimanjaro?

Kilimanjaro is a trek, not a technical climb — no ropes, ice axes or mountaineering skills are needed. What it asks for is reasonable fitness, warm gear, and above all time to acclimatise. Women of all ages summit successfully; the single biggest factor in reaching the top is choosing a longer route and a gentle, "pole pole" (slowly, slowly) pace.

The climbing routes

  • Machame ("Whiskey") route: scenic and popular, 6–7 days, with excellent acclimatisation — our usual choice.
  • Lemosho route: quieter and beautiful, 7–8 days, with the highest success rates.
  • Marangu ("Coca-Cola") route: the only hut-based route, 5–6 days, but with lower summit success.
  • Rongai route: approaches from the drier north, good in the shoulder seasons.

Best time to climb

The clearest, safest windows are the dry seasons: January to mid-March and June to October. January–March tends to be quieter and can offer snowier summit views; June–October is the long dry season and the busiest. We avoid the long rains (April–May). See our best time to visit Tanzania guide for more.

Climb & safari together

The most rewarding way to experience the mountain is to pair the trek with a safari — celebrate your summit with the elephants of Tarangire and the wildlife of the Ngorongoro Crater. Our Kilimanjaro & the Wild North itinerary does exactly that, fully supported from arrival to summit to bush.

Prefer to admire it?

You don't have to climb to be moved by Kilimanjaro. Its snows form the backdrop to game drives in nearby Amboseli (just across the Kenyan border) and glow pink at dawn from the lodges of the northern circuit. Gentle day hikes in the foothills, Chagga villages and coffee farms make a lovely, low-altitude alternative.
