Zanzibar: A Woman's Guide

Spice-scented Stone Town, dhow sails and barefoot Indian Ocean beaches.

Home / Tanzania / Zanzibar

Zanzibar is where a Tanzania safari exhales — a spice island of turquoise water, white sand and a thousand years of Swahili, Arab and Indian history layered into the coral lanes of Stone Town. It's the perfect place to slow down after the savanna, and a joy for women travelling solo, with friends or as a couple.

Why visit Zanzibar?

Part of Tanzania but a world of its own, Zanzibar blends culture and beach in a way few places manage. Mornings exploring Stone Town's markets and waterfront, afternoons under a palm with a book, dhows sailing past at sunset — it rounds off a safari beautifully, and stands proudly as a destination in its own right.

Stone Town

A UNESCO World Heritage Site, Stone Town is a maze of narrow lanes, carved wooden doors, bustling bazaars and call-to-prayer rooftops. Wander with a guide to the Old Fort and the former slave market, browse the spice and fabric stalls, and watch the sun go down over the Forodhani gardens as the street-food grills fire up.

The beaches

  • North coast (Nungwi & Kendwa): the best swimming beaches, least affected by the tides, with lively sunsets.
  • East coast (Paje, Jambiani): wide white sand, a laid-back vibe and world-class kitesurfing.
  • Offshore: Mnemba Atoll for snorkelling and diving, plus quiet private-island escapes.

Things to do

Beyond the sand: a hands-on spice-farm tour and Swahili cooking class; a traditional dhow cruise to a sandbank; snorkelling the coral gardens of Mnemba; and a morning at Jozani Forest to meet the rare red colobus monkeys found nowhere else on earth.

Best time to visit & what to wear

The sunniest, driest months are June–October and December–February; we avoid the long rains of April–May. Zanzibar is predominantly Muslim, so in Stone Town and villages we suggest covering shoulders and knees out of respect — beachwear is perfectly fine at the resorts. See our best time to visit Tanzania guide for the full calendar.

Where to stay & how to get there

We pair a boutique hotel in Stone Town with a beachfront retreat on the coast, matched to the vibe you're after. Most guests fly in by light aircraft straight from the Serengeti, or a short hop from Arusha or Dar es Salaam.
