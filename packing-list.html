Safari Packing List

What to bring (and what to leave at home) for an East Africa journey.

Home / Packing list

Packing for safari is mostly about packing light, in soft, neutral layers — and bringing a few well-chosen extras. Internal bush flights usually limit you to 15–20 kg in a soft duffel, and most camps offer same-day laundry, so you need far less than you think. Here's our women's checklist.

What to wear

  • Neutral colours — khaki, olive, beige, brown (they don't show dust and don't startle wildlife).
  • Lightweight, breathable layers: long-sleeved shirts and trousers protect from sun and insects.
  • A warm fleece or jacket — dawn game drives and the highlands can be genuinely cold.
  • A wide-brimmed hat, a scarf or buff, and a swimsuit for the pool or the coast.
  • A smart-casual outfit for elegant lodges and town dinners.

Footwear

  • Comfortable, closed walking shoes or light trainers for everyday game drives.
  • Sturdy, broken-in hiking boots if you're gorilla, chimp or Kilimanjaro trekking.
  • Sandals or flip-flops for camp and the beach.

Gorilla & forest-trek essentials

For Bwindi, the Volcanoes, Kibale and Nyungwe, the forest is steep, wet and stinging. Pack a lightweight waterproof jacket, gardening or trekking gloves (for grabbing vegetation), long trousers you can tuck into your socks, gaiters if you have them, and a small daypack. A walking pole helps, and a porter (hired locally) carries the rest.

Health & toiletries

  • High-SPF sunscreen, lip balm and good sunglasses — the equatorial sun is strong.
  • Insect repellent (DEET or picaridin) and any antimalarial medication from your doctor.
  • A personal first-aid kit, hand sanitiser and any prescription medicines in their original packaging.
  • Biodegradable toiletries and wet wipes; refillable water bottle (camps provide filtered water).

Documents & money

  • Passport valid for 6+ months with blank pages; visas and your yellow-fever certificate.
  • Printed and digital copies of your itinerary, insurance and bookings.
  • US dollars (newer notes) for tips and incidentals, plus a card; small denominations are handy.

Photography & extras

  • Camera with a zoom lens, spare batteries and memory cards; binoculars are a game-changer.
  • A universal adapter and a power bank — bush camps may only charge in communal areas.
  • A headtorch, a good book, and a reusable tote for day trips.

What NOT to bring

Leave camouflage clothing at home — it is reserved for the military and is illegal for civilians in Uganda, Tanzania, Zimbabwe and several other African countries. Skip single-use plastics and plastic bags (banned in Kenya, Rwanda and others), bright white or very dark clothing for game drives, and hard suitcases for trips with bush flights. Less really is more.
