Akagera: A Woman's Guide

Rwanda's Big Five savanna, restored along papyrus lakes and rolling plains.

Home / Rwanda / Akagera

On Rwanda's eastern border lies a different world from the misty volcanoes — Akagera, a sweep of golden savanna, papyrus swamp and shimmering lakes that is now, once again, full of big game. Its remarkable recovery is one of Africa's great conservation success stories.

Why visit Akagera?

Akagera National Park gives Rwanda its classic safari. Brought back from the brink through a partnership between the government and African Parks, it has been refenced, restocked and made safe — lions returned in 2015 and rhinos in 2017 — so it now offers genuine Big Five game viewing in beautiful, lake-strewn country just a couple of hours from Kigali.

Wildlife you'll see

  • The Big Five: lion, leopard, elephant, buffalo and reintroduced black and white rhino.
  • Plains & water: giraffe, zebra, hippo, crocodile and large herds of antelope.
  • Birds: nearly 500 species, including the prized, prehistoric-looking shoebill in the wetlands.

The boat safari

One of Akagera's great pleasures is a boat safari on Lake Ihema, drifting past pods of hippo, basking crocodiles and waterbirds with elephant and buffalo coming down to drink. Combined with morning and evening game drives, it makes for a rounded, relaxed safari.

Best time to visit

Game viewing is good year-round, but the drier months — June–September and December–February — concentrate wildlife around the lakes and make the tracks easiest. The green seasons are lush, scenic and superb for birding. See our best time to visit Rwanda guide for more.

Where to stay & how to get there

We favour intimate lakeside camps inside the park, from luxurious tented suites to relaxed eco-tents on the shore of Lake Ihema. Akagera is an easy two-to-three-hour drive east from Kigali, which makes it a natural finale — or a wild contrast to a gorilla trek. We arrange every transfer and the boat safari.
