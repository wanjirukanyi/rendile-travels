Kibale Forest: A Woman's Guide

The chimpanzee capital of East Africa, alive with thirteen primate species.

Home / Uganda / Kibale Forest

If Bwindi belongs to the gorillas, Kibale belongs to the chimps. This tall, green tropical forest holds the highest density of primates in East Africa — and tracking a community of wild chimpanzees through the canopy, following their hoots and drumming, is every bit as thrilling as it sounds.

Why visit Kibale?

Kibale National Park protects one of the loveliest and most accessible rainforests in Uganda, home to thirteen primate species and around 1,500 chimpanzees. It pairs naturally with Queen Elizabeth next door, and its mix of forest walks, wetland birding and warm community tourism makes it a rich, gentle few days.

Chimpanzee trekking

After a morning briefing you set off with a ranger into the forest, guided by the chimps' calls. When you find them, you have an hour in their company — youngsters swinging overhead, adults grooming and feeding, the whole community noisy and utterly absorbing. For the truly keen, an all-day chimpanzee habituation experience offers even longer.

Wildlife & the Bigodi wetland

  • Primates galore: red colobus, black-and-white colobus, grey-cheeked mangabey, L'Hoest's and more.
  • Bigodi swamp walk: a community-run wetland trail famous for monkeys and over 200 bird species.
  • Forest elephants & birds: shy forest elephants pass through, and the birding is superb.

Best time to visit

Chimpanzee permits run year-round, but the drier months — June–September and December–February — make the forest trails easier underfoot. The wetter seasons are greener and quieter. See our best time to visit Uganda guide for more.

Where to stay & how to get there

We favour characterful lodges in the crater-lake country around the forest, several with views over the hills and warm, personal service. Kibale is an easy drive from Queen Elizabeth, or reached by light aircraft and road from Entebbe. It slots beautifully into a gorillas-and-chimps or grand-loop itinerary. We arrange every leg.
